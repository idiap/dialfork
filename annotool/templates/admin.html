📦 Batches

How batches work

A batch is conversations of one task, in mini-blocks of 5 (about 2.5 minutes per conversation, so 20 is about 50 minutes). Nothing is fixed in advance: start from zero, make batches as people become available, delete the ones you do not need.

What to do: give each person one or two tasks — one batch per task. Someone who keeps judging against the same workflow gets familiar with it and faster at spotting deviations. Choosing for <person> draws the batch around what that person already holds and gives it to them straight away. For agreement, give some batches to a second person as well — ideally different pairings.

Why batches are built the way they are. Two properties, both load-bearing:

  • Every mini-block of 5 is balanced: one fork of each hallucination type, one at each of the four fork positions, plus 1 conformant conversation; four of them make a block of 20 with each type at each position once. People get them one mini-block at a time, so whatever is finished is a balanced sample and collection can stop at any point.
  • Nobody sees two versions of the same source dialogue — not in one batch, and not across their batches. The versions share the dialogue's opening, so comparing them would give away where the fork is. That is why some people are sometimes not offered a batch; the row says who and why.

A batch needs as many source dialogues as it has conversations, so the two smallest tasks (ride_status: 9, ride_change: 13) can only supply small ones. When choosing tasks, mixing short, medium and long ones (mean assistant turns shown in the list) and different domains keeps the study representative. Decide the task list and the stopping rule before collecting.
